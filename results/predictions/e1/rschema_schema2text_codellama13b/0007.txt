The database name is rschema_0007 and it is used for storing information about advertising clients, advertisements, and client broadcast records. The database has three tables: advertising_client, advertisement, and client_broadcast_record.
The advertising_client table has four columns: client_id, name, phone, and email. The client_id column is the primary key and is a unique identifier for each client. The name, phone, and email columns are all required and contain the client's name, phone number, and email address, respectively.
The advertisement table has six columns: advertisement_id, name, broadcast_time, duration, price, and client_id. The advertisement_id column is the primary key and is a unique identifier for each advertisement. The name, broadcast_time, duration, and price columns are all required and contain the advertisement's name, broadcast time, duration, and price, respectively. The client_id column is a foreign key that references the client_id column in the advertising_client table.
The client_broadcast_record table has five columns: advertisement_id, client_id, broadcast_time, broadcast_channel, and operating_personnel. The client_id and advertisement_id columns are the primary key and are a composite key that uniquely identifies each client broadcast record. The broadcast_time, broadcast_channel, and operating_personnel columns are all required and contain the time of the broadcast, the broadcast channel, and the name of the person responsible for the broadcast, respectively. The client_id column is a foreign key that references the client_id column in the advertising_client table, and the advertisement_id column is a foreign key that references the advertisement_id column in the advertisement table.
In summary, the rschema_0007 database is used to store information about advertising clients, advertisements, and client broadcast records. The database has three tables that are related through foreign keys, and each table has a primary key and other required columns. The database ensures data integrity through entity and referential integrity constraints, such as primary keys, foreign keys, and unique constraints.
