This database schema, named rschema_0272, is used to store information about livestock and their health, vaccination status, feed consumption, and entry/exit records. It contains six tables: Ranch, Livestock, Health_Status, Vaccination_Status, Feed_Consumption, and Entry/Exit_Records.
The Ranch table contains information about the ranch, including the address, name, and ranch ID. The Livestock table contains information about the livestock, including the livestock ID, name, species, and the ranch ID. The Health_Status table contains information about the health status of the livestock, including the health status, livestock ID, and recording time. The Vaccination_Status table contains information about the vaccination status of the livestock, including the vaccination measures, vaccination time, and livestock ID. The Feed_Consumption table contains information about the feed consumption of the livestock, including the consumption amount, consumption time, feed type, and livestock ID. The Entry/Exit_Records table contains information about the entry and exit records of the livestock, including the entry/exit type, livestock ID, and time.
Each table has primary keys and foreign keys to enforce entity and referential integrity. The primary keys are unique identifiers for each table, and the foreign keys reference the primary keys of other tables to establish relationships between the tables. The foreign keys also have update and delete rules, such as CASCADE and RESTRICT, to ensure data consistency and integrity.
In addition to the tables, the schema also includes indexes and constraints to optimize query performance and enforce data consistency. The indexes are used to speed up queries by allowing the database to quickly locate the data, while the constraints ensure that the data is consistent and accurate.
Overall, this database schema is designed to store and manage information about livestock and their health, vaccination status, feed consumption, and entry/exit records, while ensuring data consistency and integrity through the use of primary keys, foreign keys, and indexes.
