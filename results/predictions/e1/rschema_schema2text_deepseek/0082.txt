The database schema named 'rschema_0082' comprises three tables: 'political_party', 'candidate', and 'voter'.
The 'political_party' table represents the parties involved in the election. It has four columns: 'party_id' (a unique identifier for each party), 'party_name' (the name of the party), 'party_description' (a brief description of the party's purpose), and 'party_id' as the primary key.
The 'candidate' table represents the candidates running for election. It has five columns: 'candidate_id' (a unique identifier for each candidate), 'name' (the candidate's name), 'belonging_party_id' (the party the candidate belongs to), 'industry_connection' (the industry the candidate is connected to), and 'candidate_id' as the primary key. The 'belonging_party_id' column is a foreign key referencing the 'party_id' in the 'political_party' table, ensuring referential integrity.
Finally, the 'voter' table represents the voters who have the right to vote. It has six columns: 'voter_id' (a unique identifier for each voter), 'name' (the voter's name), 'email' (the voter's email), 'creation_time' (the time the voter registered), 'voted_candidate_id' (the candidate the voter voted for), 'vote_time' (the time the voter cast their vote), and 'voter_id' as the primary key. The 'candidate_id' column is a foreign key referencing the 'candidate_id' in the 'candidate' table, ensuring referential integrity. The 'candidate_id' in the 'voter' table allows voters to vote for a specific candidate, maintaining entity integrity.
The 'voter' table has 'candidate_id' as a foreign key, which on delete and update cascades, meaning if a candidate is deleted or updated, the corresponding entries in the 'voter' table will also be deleted or updated, maintaining referential integrity.
