The database schema named 'rschema_0101' consists of five tables.
The 'employee' table is used to store information about the employees of the organization. It includes columns such as 'employee_id', 'name', 'gender', 'date_of_birth', 'phone', 'id_number', 'place_of_origin', 'age', 'department_id', 'position', 'level', and 'contract_type'. The 'employee_id' column is the primary key of this table.
The 'department' table holds information about the departments within the organization. It includes columns such as 'department_id', 'department_name', 'department_responsibilities', 'department_head_id', 'total_number_of_people_in_the_department', and 'department_location'. The 'department_id' is the primary key of this table and a foreign key 'department_head_id' references the 'employee_id' in the 'employee' table.
The 'employee_department_transfer' table keeps track of the department transfers of the employees. It includes columns such as 'employee_id', 'transfer-in_department_id', 'transfer-out_department_id', 'transfer_time', and 'post-transfer_position'. The combination of 'transfer_time' and 'employee_id' is the primary key of this table and foreign keys 'employee_id', 'transfer-in_department_id', and 'transfer-out_department_id' reference the 'employee_id' and 'department_id' in the 'employee' and 'department' tables respectively.
The 'employee_attendance' table records the attendance information of the employees. It includes columns such as 'employee_id', 'punch-in_time', and 'punch-in_type'. The combination of 'employee_id' and 'punch-in_time' is the primary key of this table and the 'employee_id' references the 'employee_id' in the 'employee' table.
Finally, the 'performance_evaluation' table is used to store the performance evaluations of the employees. It includes columns such as 'employee_id', 'evaluation_time', 'evaluation_content', 'evaluation_result', and 'evaluator_id'. The combination of 'employee_id' and 'evaluation_time' is the primary key of this table and foreign keys 'employee_id' and 'evaluator_id' reference the 'employee_id' in the 'employee' table.
All these tables have been set up with appropriate constraints to maintain data integrity. The primary keys ensure entity integrity, the foreign keys enforce referential integrity, and the constraints ensure that the data in the tables adheres to certain rules.
