The schemabench_0136 database is a collection of five tables that represent various aspects of a university system. The tables are designed to enforce data integrity and relationships between them.
The first table, RA, represents the relationship between professors and students. It has four columns: capability, prof_id, student_id, and salary. The capability column represents the professor's ability to teach the course, while the prof_id and student_id columns are foreign keys that reference the prof and student tables. The salary column is optional and represents the professor's salary. The primary key of this table is a composite key consisting of prof_id and student_id.
The second table, course, represents the courses offered by the university. It has three columns: course_id, rating, and diff. The course_id column is the primary key, and the rating and diff columns represent the course's difficulty and rating.
The third table, prof, represents the professors in the university. It has three columns: prof_id, popularity, and teachingability. The prof_id column is the primary key, and the popularity and teachingability columns represent the professor's popularity and teaching ability.
The fourth table, registration, represents the students' registration for courses. It has four columns: course_id, student_id, grade, and sat. The course_id and student_id columns are foreign keys that reference the course and student tables, respectively. The grade and sat columns represent the student's grade and SAT score for the course. The primary key of this table is a composite key consisting of course_id and student_id.
The fifth table, student, represents the students in the university. It has three columns: student_id, intelligence, and ranking. The student_id column is the primary key, and the intelligence and ranking columns represent the student's intelligence and ranking.
In summary, the schemabench_0136 database is a comprehensive system that encompasses various aspects of a university, including the relationship between professors and students, the courses offered, the professors, the students' registration, and the students themselves. The tables are designed to enforce data integrity and relationships between them, ensuring that the data is accurate and consistent.
