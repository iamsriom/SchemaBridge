This schema is for a restaurant management system. It contains seven tables: Customers, Staff, Bookings, Orders, Orders_Delivery_Status, Menu, and Orders_Details.
The Customers table stores information about the customers who make bookings at the restaurant. It has three columns: Customer_ID, Full_Name, and Phone_Number. Customer_ID is the primary key and is not null. Full_Name and Phone_Number are also not null.
The Staff table stores information about the staff members who work at the restaurant. It has seven columns: Staff_ID, Name, Role, Salary, Address, Contact_Number, and Email. Staff_ID is the primary key and is not null. Name, Role, Salary, Address, Contact_Number, and Email are also not null.
The Bookings table stores information about the bookings made by customers. It has six columns: Booking_ID, Booking_Date, Customer_ID, Table_number, Number_of_Persons, and Staff_ID. Booking_ID is the primary key and is not null. Booking_Date, Customer_ID, Table_number, Number_of_Persons, and Staff_ID are also not null.
The Orders table stores information about the orders placed by customers. It has five columns: Order_ID, Table_number, Order_Date, Total_Cost, and Booking_ID. Order_ID is the primary key and is not null. Table_number, Order_Date, Total_Cost, and Booking_ID are also not null.
The Orders_Delivery_Status table stores information about the delivery status of orders. It has three columns: Order_ID, Delivery_Date, and Delivery_Status. Order_ID is the primary key and is not null. Delivery_Date and Delivery_Status are also not null.
The Menu table stores information about the menu items available at the restaurant. It has five columns: Item_ID, Name, Category, Cuisine, and Price. Item_ID is the primary key and is not null. Name, Category, Cuisine, and Price are also not null.
The Orders_Details table stores information about the items ordered by customers. It has three columns: OrderID, Item_ID, and Quantity. OrderID and Item_ID are the primary keys and are not null. Quantity is also not null.
The foreign keys in the schema are used to establish relationships between the tables. For example, the Bookings table has a foreign key to the Customers table, which references the Customer_ID column in the Customers table. The Orders table has a foreign key to the Bookings table, which references the Booking_ID column in the Bookings table. The Orders_Details table has foreign keys to the Menu and Orders tables, which reference the Item_ID and Order_ID columns in those tables, respectively.
The schema also includes primary keys and unique constraints to ensure the integrity of the data. For example, the Customer_ID column in the Customers table is the primary key, and the Booking_ID column in the Bookings table is the primary key. The Item_ID column in the Menu table is the primary key, and the Order_ID column in the Orders table is the primary key.
In summary, this schema is used to manage the bookings, orders, and delivery status of customers at a restaurant. It includes tables for customers, staff, bookings, orders, orders delivery status, menu items, and orders details. The schema uses foreign keys and primary keys to establish relationships between the tables and ensure data integrity.
